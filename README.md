# Digital Thorium

**A Market Architecture for India's Thorium Century**

*The Convergence of Reserves, Compute, and Ownership*

By Abhilash Inumella and Anubhav Tiwari · April 2026

---

## What this is

A five-part policy package proposing that India create a tradable, fractional, on-chain claim on a unit of thorium-derived electricity. The instrument — Digital Thorium — is denominated in **kilowatt-hour-years (kWhy)**, issued by a federal vehicle sitting between the Department of Atomic Energy and SEBI, and designed to let ordinary Indians own the upside of the country's thorium build-out.

The thesis rests on three convergences:

1. **Reserves.** India holds ~846,477 tonnes of thorium metal — roughly 25% of the known global inventory. The Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor at Kalpakkam achieved first criticality on 6 April 2026.
2. **Demand.** Global data-centre electricity demand is projected to more than double from 415 TWh (2024) to ~945 TWh (2030), and ~2,000 TWh by 2035 in the lift-off case. Electrons are the new oil; compute is the new refinery.
3. **Ownership.** In the coming decade more people will want a stake in energy than in land, because owning energy increasingly means owning intelligence.

## The instrument in one paragraph

One token = one **kilowatt-hour-year**: a claim on the delivery (or rupee-settled monetary equivalent) of 1 kWh per year, for the contract duration, from a specified certified reactor cohort. Issuance is federal via the proposed **National Thorium Finance Corporation (NTFC)**. Holders get cash settlement quarterly at the wholesale clearing price, physical take-up above a threshold size, and secondary trading on a regulated exchange. The token layer is a permissioned ledger — legal certainty, not censorship resistance. The architecture is intentionally post-crypto: token is the database entry, settlement is rupee, enforcement is Indian law, promise is electricity.

## Protocol stack

| Layer | Function | Primary Regulator | Failure Mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | Thorium extraction, fuel fabrication, reactor operations | AERB, DAE | Capacity shortfall |
| Settlement | PPA enforcement, rupee payment, grid metering | CERC, POSOCO | Price suppression |
| Token | Issuance, custody, transfer, redemption | SEBI, NTFC | Counterparty fraud |
| Derivative | Futures, options, structured products | SEBI, RBI | Speculative blowout |

## Headline numbers (central case)

- **Launch price:** ₹20 per kWhy · **Minimum ticket:** ₹500
- **Target real IRR:** 11.2% · **Nominal IRR:** 14.5% · **Distribution yield (launch):** ~5.0%
- **Modified duration:** ~12.6 years · **Macaulay:** ~14.0 years · **Asset life:** 60 years
- **Net pass-through to holders:** 87.5% of gross power sales (statutory cap: 15% deduction)
- **Build-out target:** 50 GW thorium-cycle capacity over 20 years (~$250B capex)
- **Steady-state output:** ~359 TWh/year at 82% capacity factor (~21% of India's 2025 generation)
- **Tokenisable equity:** ~$30B / ~₹2.5 lakh crore over 20 years
- **Retail reach:** up to ~200 million households at ₹500 minimum ticket
- **Capital formation velocity:** ~8× acceleration vs. the state-monopoly baseline of 0.3 GW/year
- **Programme sizing:** 500 MW pilot (2027) → 5 GW (2030) → 20 GW (2035) → 50 GW commissioned (2046)
