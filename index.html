Owning India's Electrons

India holds a quarter of the world's thorium. The world's appetite for electricity is about to triple. This is a proposal for how ordinary Indians could own a slice of the convergence.

My grandfather kept his savings in gold. My father kept his savings in property. I do not yet know what to do with mine. There is a third answer becoming available, and it sits on two facts: India holds ~846,000 tonnes of thorium, and global data-centre electricity demand is projected to rise from 415 TWh in 2024 to ~2,000 TWh by 2035.

What is proposed is a way to own a slice of that. The instrument is called Digital Thorium. One token = one kilowatt-hour-year of claim on a federally-supervised reactor cohort. Minimum ticket: ₹500. Distributions land in your bank account every 90 days.

Caveats

This is not investment advice. Digital Thorium is a policy proposal. The instrument has not launched. The proposal would require Parliament, with implementing regulations from SEBI and the RBI. Final terms will be set in cohort-specific prospectuses at issuance.

Numbers shown in the simulator and dashboard mockup are illustrative, derived from the central case in the policy report (target nominal IRR 14.5%, distribution yield 5% at launch, 1.5% real escalation). Comparison rates for FDs, Nifty 50, and gold reflect long-run nominal averages and are not guarantees. Real outcomes depend on reactor commissioning on schedule, AI demand projections holding, and the political durability of the statutory framework. Each of these can disappoint.

In the worst plausible scenario, the holder still earns roughly bank fixed deposit returns on a productive real asset. The floor is not zero, but the central case is not a promise either.
